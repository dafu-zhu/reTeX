\section{Discussion and Bibliography}
\label{sec:5.4}

The Gibbs sampler was introduced in \cite{82}. The paper \cite{80} further popularized the
approach, making apparent its usefulness for Bayesian inference. The derivation of full
conditionals for Gaussians in Example~\ref{ex:5.2} can be found for instance in \cite{206}.
Example~\ref{ex:5.6} briefly introduced the slice sampler for univariate targets. We refer
to \cite{169} for multivariate extensions and to \cite{166} for slice samplers for function
space sampling. A comparison between random and deterministic updates can be found in
\cite{8}. Simple conditions on the target to guarantee convergence were established in
\cite{202}, and geometric ergodicity of the Gibbs sampler was further studied in \cite{197}.
The proof of convergence for Gaussian targets is taken from \cite{201}, which also contains
fundamental results for the understanding of blocking and other variants of the Gibbs sampler.
Gibbs samplers for hierarchical models have been widely studied, see e.g.\ \cite{183} and
references therein. Finally, adaptive Gibbs samplers are investigated in \cite{48}.
